Overview

I founded and led an AIAA DBF-style aircraft subteam within the EvCC STEM Club, choosing a learn-by-building approach over immediately targeting competition requirements.

Oct 2024

Team formed. Aerodynamic sizing, airfoil selection, and wing geometry established.

Nov 2024

A small-scale airplane was designed in SolidWorks and built with PLA. Flight testing revealed the CG was positioned aft of the center of lift, and the vertical stabilizer separated from the fuselage on the second flight.

Early 2025

Design pivoted to a rib-and-spar construction. Final configuration confirmed: 8 PLA ribs on carbon fiber spars, Depron foam skin, all-moving horizontal and vertical stabilizers, full-span ailerons on ribs 2 through 4 and 7 through 9, and a bolted wing-fuselage joint. Full-scale SolidWorks CAD started and small-scale wing ribs printed in PLA to validate the rib profile. I left the team at this point to transfer to the University of Washington.

Problem & Approach

The team started with almost no prior experience in RC aircraft design. Rather than working directly toward AIAA DBF competition requirements, the decision was to build and fly something first to develop foundational understanding before refining toward competition standards.

The first problem was figuring out where to start. Early team meetings focused on understanding basic aerodynamic relationships including lift, drag, Reynolds number, aspect ratio, and wing loading before any design decisions were made. Sizing parameters were established through whiteboard calculations: a 6-foot wingspan, 7.5 aspect ratio, 9.61-inch chord, 611 in² wing area, and a cruise speed of 20 mph giving a Reynolds number of approximately 1.04 x 10⁵.

Airfoil selection settled on a NACA 2411-style profile based on the low Reynolds number regime and the team's manufacturing capability. The construction approach used 3D-printed PLA ribs on carbon fiber spars with Depron foam skin, chosen because it was manufacturable with the tools and budget available. A small-scale foam model was built first to validate the general configuration before committing to the full structural design.

My Role

My primary contribution in the early phase was organizational. The team had no prior aircraft design experience and no established workflow, so a significant portion of my time went into structuring the team, setting design review cadences, and establishing a starting point for the technical work. This took longer than expected and delayed the actual design phase.

Leadership

  • Founded the aircraft subteam within the EvCC STEM Club and recruited 7 members across Aeronautics and Mechanical tracks.
  • Established team structure, meeting cadence, and documentation workflow from scratch. A significant portion of early project time went into organizing the team rather than technical work, which delayed the design phase.

Technical

  • Led aerodynamic sizing including wingspan, chord, aspect ratio, wing area, Reynolds number, and cruise speed target.
  • Drove airfoil selection toward NACA 2411-style profile based on the low Reynolds number regime and team manufacturing capability.
  • Led the structural design of the rib-and-spar wing, defining the 8-rib layout, carbon fiber rod spar connections, Depron foam skin approach, wing-fuselage bolted joint, full-span aileron configuration across ribs 2 through 4 and 7 through 9, and all-moving horizontal and vertical stabilizer geometry.
  • Printed small-scale PLA wing ribs to validate the rib profile before leaving the team to transfer to the University of Washington.

Limitations

This project did not reach full-scale build or flight. The following limitations reflect honest constraints from the project and inform the reconstruction in the next section.
