Overview

This project was part of the Everett Community College STEM Club Rocket Team's high-power rocketry work, oriented toward ESRA IREC competition preparation. The team designed and built a 6-ft high-power rocket intended to carry an 8.8-lb payload nearly two miles and prepared for a June 2024 launch at an OROC launch site in Brothers, Oregon. The project did not reach official IREC registration or competition entry since our flight was not sucessful, rather, this page focuses on my fabrication work, OpenRocket simulation contributions, and a reconstructed technical review of the launch-readiness limitations.

Problem & Approach

Our project started with the IREC competition requirements: build a flight vehicle capable of reaching 10,000 feet with an 8.8-pound payload, perform a dual-deploy recovery sequence, and maintain GPS tracking throughout the entire flight. The hard part was meeting all three requirements at once while keeping the vehicle stable. Stability shifts as the motor burns and mass drops, so we modeled the CG/CP relationship across payload, recovery, and motor configurations in OpenRocket and upgraded from a K-class to an M-class motor when the thrust numbers did not add up for the target altitude with payload on board. On the hardware side, a Featherweight GPS tracker handled telemetry and a dual-deploy altimeter managed the two-stage recovery sequence, deploying the drogue at apogee and the main parachute at lower altitude. The airframe consisted of carbon fiber tubing with fiberglass couplers and bulkheads, epoxy bonding throughout, and a Delrin nose cone.

System Architecture

The vehicle system included the nose cone, airframe, motor configuration, recovery system, payload considerations, and stability relationship between center of gravity and center of pressure.

My Role

  • Estimated center of gravity and center of pressure across configurations.
  • Simulated apogee sensitivity to mass distribution changes.
  • Supported MATLAB and OpenRocket analysis.
  • Fabricated and assembled hardware including nose cone and airframe components.
